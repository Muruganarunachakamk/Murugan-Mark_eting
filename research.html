PROPOSED WIP ARCHITECTURE
SECTION 4 · THE FOUR RESEARCH GAPS

Why existing chatbot studies fall short

  • Gap 1 (Outcome Isolation): Prior work terminates at general trust or satisfaction rather than isolating state-like, interaction-specific situational consumer skepticism.
  • Gap 2 (Single-Mechanism Bias): Warmth, competence, privacy concern, and authenticity are typically tested in isolation, risking omitted-variable bias.
  • Gap 3 (Context Integration): No identified study integrates all four psychological mechanisms into one model of situational skepticism in e-commerce customer support.
  • Gap 4 (Untested Serial Extension): Experimental evidence shows Competence → Authenticity (Nguyen et al., 2023), but has not tested whether that sequence extends to situational consumer skepticism.
SECTION 6 · FIVE THEORETICAL FOUNDATIONS

Multi-theory architecture

  • 1. CASA Paradigm: Explains why human-like chatbot cues trigger automatic social evaluation rules.
  • 2. Stereotype Content Model (SCM): Organizes social judgment along two parallel axes: Warmth (intent) and Competence (ability).
  • 3. Privacy Calculus: Explains the risk-appraisal pathway where human-like personalization raises data surveillance concerns.
  • 4. Social Response Theory: Grounds H3 — functional competence serves as a precondition for judging human-like persona cues as authentic rather than manipulative.
  • 5. Persuasion Knowledge Model (PKM): Explains why consumers actively question whether an AI bot is genuinely helping or acting as a cost-cutting barrier.

Section 7.5: The 8-Stage Model Evolution Traceability +
  1. Stage 1 (Broad Topic): Role of Anthropomorphic Chatbots in Reducing Consumer Skepticism toward AI-Based Customer Support.
  2. Stage 2 (Competence Added): Identified Perceived Competence as the most consistently evidenced functional transmitter (Blut et al., 2021).
  3. Stage 3 (Warmth Added in Parallel): Incorporated Perceived Warmth via the Stereotype Content Model so social evaluation was not one-dimensional.
  4. Stage 4 (DV Sharpened): Refined general skepticism to Situational Consumer Skepticism to isolate state-like interaction doubt.
  5. Stage 5 (Bounded to E-Commerce): Scoped to e-commerce support (refunds, delayed orders) where profit-motive suspicion is salient.
  6. Stage 6 (Privacy & Authenticity Added): Added Privacy Concerns (risk pathway) and Perceived Authenticity (sincerity pathway).
  7. Stage 7 (Competence → Authenticity Sequence Identified): Incorporated replicated experimental evidence (Nguyen et al., 2023) that competence predicts authenticity.
  8. Stage 8 (Current Hybrid Model): Retained strictly parallel paths for Warmth, Competence, and Privacy while adding the single Competence → Authenticity serial link.
Sections 14 & 15: Planned Experimental Design & 13-Section Questionnaire Architecture +

Between-Subjects Scenario Experiment: Respondents (planned target N = 400–500, ~200–250 per condition) will be randomly assigned to a Higher-Anthropomorphism condition (human name, avatar, conversational first-person language) or Lower-Anthropomorphism condition (neutral icon, formal wording) while resolving an identical e-commerce delayed-order issue.

Questionnaire Flow (Sections A–M, 38 Working Items on 7-Point Likert Scales): Section A (Consent) → Section B (Demographics) → Section C (E-Commerce & Chatbot Usage) → Section D (Experimental Scenario Exposure) → Section E (Attention Check, Item 10) → Section F (Manipulation Check: Human-Likeness, Items 11–13, adapted from Aggarwal & McGill 2007) → Section G (Perceived Warmth, Items 14–17, adapted from Xu et al. 2013) → Section H (Perceived Competence, Items 18–21) → Section I (Privacy Concerns, Items 22–25) → Section J (Perceived Authenticity, Items 26–29) → Section K (Situational Consumer Skepticism, Items 30–34) → Section L (Prior AI Experience & Controls, Items 35–37) → Section M (Overall Evaluation, Item 38).
